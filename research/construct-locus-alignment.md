## Construct-by-construct reasoning

### 1. Competence Development Practices — strongest field alignment

The Renstra does more than mention training. It identifies an instructor-professionalism weakness and specifies an architecture of capability development including task-responsive Latharkatpuan, curriculum improvement, competence certification, certified instructors, personalized learning, rotation, and performance-linked HR management. This makes the **organizational development-practice domain** highly salient in the locus.

The construct is nevertheless not fully locked because the academic definition must be narrower than the entire HR system. If X2 becomes an omnibus variable containing training, rotation, career management, certification, reward, and every development activity, construct validity will deteriorate. Issue #10 must determine the defensible boundary.

### 2. Leadership — real problem, specific leadership style still open

Leadership is not a speculative locus issue. The Renstra explicitly records weakness in leadership across development, operations, control, and supervision. The unresolved question is **what academic construct best represents that problem**. Transformational leadership may fit parts of the issue, particularly development, inspiration, individualized consideration, and intellectual stimulation, but the documented weakness also contains command/control and supervision content that may not be uniquely transformational.

Therefore the dissertation may retain leadership as a core domain while requiring direct justification before locking `Transformational Leadership` specifically.

### 3. Individual Work Performance — relevant endpoint, not a documented deficit

The dissertation requires an individual-level outcome if it is to remain an HRM study of personnel mechanisms. Individual work performance is a coherent candidate, supported by Indonesian measurement evidence in `SRC-012`. But Renstra targets cannot be treated as its measurement. Organizational operational effectiveness, certification percentages, SAKIP, budget performance, and program completion are different constructs and levels.

The background should therefore frame a **translation problem**—how organizational development/leadership conditions are reflected in personnel-level work outcomes—not a generalized low-performance crisis.

### 4. Digital Competence — contextually real, structurally unresolved

Technology capability is directly present in the Renstra and in current capability-building activity. The main vulnerability is not relevance but **scope and role**. Some Brimob functions visibly have high digital/technology demand while others may rely on different capability profiles. A universal digital-competence moderator would be weak if the sampled jobs differ radically in digital task intensity.

Before construct lock, the project must decide whether digital competence is:

- a common capability across the target population;
- a role-specific capability requiring a bounded population;
- a main predictor/control/context variable;
- a moderator with a defensible mechanism;
- or better represented by a task-specific technology competence construct.

### 5. Affective Organizational Commitment — mechanism candidate with the weakest direct field evidence

AOC currently has the weakest direct connection to the documented field problem. The Renstra does not identify an attachment/commitment deficit and, conversely, describes esprit de corps, discipline, loyalty, and dedication as strengths. This does not falsify AOC as a mechanism because institutional statements are not employee-level AOC measurement, but it removes any basis for claiming that low AOC is the practical problem.

The literature registered so far also cautions against treating AOC as the only mechanism: employee engagement, trust, and other relational/psychological mechanisms appear in the closest studies. AOC should survive only if Issue #11 shows that it explains the locus-relevant process better than plausible alternatives and Issue #9 shows it is observable/researchable at personnel level.

## Cross-level risks

1. **Organizational diagnosis → individual construct:** Renstra weaknesses are organizational self-diagnosis and cannot establish personnel-level prevalence.
2. **Program existence → employee experience:** the presence of Latharkatpuan/certification does not establish equal access, perceived quality, or effectiveness.
3. **Leadership weakness → leadership style:** an organization-level weakness label does not identify TL specifically.
4. **Technology strategy → universal digital competence:** role-specific requirements may create severe construct heterogeneity.
5. **Strategic performance → individual work performance:** Renstra indicators cannot be substituted for employee-level IWP.

## Field-validation requirements before lock

### Mandatory for Issue #9

1. Define candidate respondent population and relevant unit(s)/functions.
2. Establish the immediate-supervisor/commander referent for leadership measurement.
3. Map development-practice exposure across roles, ranks, and units.
4. Map digital task intensity and technology dependence by function.
5. Determine whether individual performance can be obtained from supervisor appraisal, administrative appraisal, self-report, or multiple sources.
6. Identify any internal survey/evaluation records that can corroborate or qualify the Renstra weaknesses at unit/personnel level.
7. Assess confidentiality, command-chain, and social-desirability constraints.

### Mandatory for Issue #10

1. Compare TL with leadership rivals rather than treating TL as default.
2. Bound competence-development practices versus training, HRD climate, POS-development, career development, and actual competence.
3. Compare AOC with engagement, identification, trust, POS, and public-service motivation.
4. Bound digital competence versus digital literacy, self-efficacy, readiness, and task-specific technical competence.
5. Bound IWP versus adaptive performance, operational readiness, unit performance, and formal appraisal.

## Preliminary recommendation for the background manuscript

The current background should **retain all five constructs only as working architecture**, with unequal epistemic status:

- Competence Development Practices can be presented as the strongest HRM construct candidate directly grounded in the Renstra.
- Leadership can be presented as a documented problem domain, while `Transformational Leadership` remains a construct choice under audit.
- Individual Work Performance can be presented as the person-level outcome candidate, not as an already documented deficit.
- Digital Competence can be presented as a capability candidate relevant to modernization, with role/task boundaries and moderator status unresolved.
- Affective Organizational Commitment can be presented only as a candidate attitudinal mechanism supported by literature, not as a Renstra-documented problem.

This wording keeps the background empirically honest and prevents later construct/model lock from requiring a complete rewrite.
