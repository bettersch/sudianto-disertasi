## Evidence retained
The study used a three-wave survey with 332 employees undergoing digital transformation. Drawing on cognitive appraisal, JD-R, and conservation-of-resources logic, it found that digital technology requirements can be appraised as challenge or hindrance/threat-like demands and that employees' digital literacy changes these appraisal processes and downstream behavioral responses.

## Research use
This study strengthens the interpretation of digital competence/literacy as a personal resource whose moderating role is most defensible when a digital requirement or digitalized work demand is explicitly present in the causal chain.

## Limits
The study does not test affective organizational commitment, competence-development practices, or the proposed AOC–performance interaction. It therefore cannot be used as direct empirical support for digital competence moderating the commitment–performance relationship.
