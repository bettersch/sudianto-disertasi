## 1. Transformational Leadership (X1)

### Working definition

If retained, Transformational Leadership should refer to **observable behavior of the respondent's immediate leader/supervisor that articulates a positive direction, develops and supports staff, empowers and intellectually stimulates followers, models desired behavior, and motivates performance beyond routine exchange**.

The definition must be anchored to a clearly observable leader referent, not abstract “leadership of Korbrimob.”

### Authoritative/measurement lineage

`SRC-015` documents the Global Transformational Leadership (GTL) scale developed by Carless, Wearing, and Mann (2000). It was developed from 1,440 subordinates rating 695 managers and supported as a concise unidimensional measure. Later applications characterize GTL as seven items referring to the immediate leader and covering vision, staff development, support, empowerment, innovation, leading by example, and charisma.

The MLQ 5X is the other established measurement family, but `SRC-020` establishes that research administration is licensed/copyright-controlled through Mind Garden and carries cost/reproduction/translation conditions. Instrument familiarity is therefore not sufficient justification for choosing MLQ.

### Rival/adjacent constructs to test

- leader/supervisor support;
- ethical leadership;
- empowering leadership;
- leader competence / command effectiveness;
- transactional leadership and active management-by-exception;
- supervisory/command climate.

### Discriminant risk in Korbrimob

The Renstra problem explicitly includes **pembinaan, operasional, pengendalian, dan pengawasan**. Some of this content may be closer to command/supervisory effectiveness than transformational behavior. A TL measure can therefore show good psychometrics and still have poor **problem–construct fit**.

### Measurement candidate

**GTL (Carless et al., 2000)** is currently the more feasible short candidate because it reduces questionnaire burden and is naturally subordinate-rated. However:

1. exact item reuse/permission must be verified;
2. Indonesian translation/adaptation must be handled formally;
3. content validity with Brimob leaders/personnel must test whether the seven behaviors represent the actual leadership problem;
4. if command/control/supervision content is central and not captured, a different or additional construct may be required.

### Status

**Leadership domain: strong. Transformational Leadership label: conditional.** Do not lock X1 as TL until Issue #11 shows theoretical superiority and Issue #9/field evidence confirms that subordinate-observable TL behavior is the relevant level.

## 2. Competence Development Practices (X2)

### Working definition

**Perceived Organizational Competence Development Practices** should mean the employee's perception of organization-provided practices/opportunities intended to develop job-relevant competence over time.

This is **not**:

- actual competence level;
- number of training sessions attended;
- training satisfaction only;
- certification status alone;
- generic HRM quality.

### Measurement lineage

`SRC-019` and the stronger later application `SRC-010` consistently represent organizational competence-development practices through three recurring domains:

1. training;
2. individualized developmental support;
3. functional rotation.

The 2024 large-sample application used this structure but also showed that item performance can vary, which cautions against copying an item set without local validation.

### Fit with Korbrimob

This construct has the strongest locus fit because Renstra contains:

- Latharkatpuan;
- curriculum relevance;
- competence certification;
- instructor capability;
- personalized learning;
- functional/role rotation;
- task-current capability development.

But the Renstra contains **more practices than the academic scale**. That is a construct-design risk.

### Rival/adjacent constructs

- perceived organizational support for development;
- HRD climate / learning climate;
- training effectiveness;
- career-development support;
- learning opportunities;
- actual competence/skill;
- high-performance work practices.

### Measurement rule

Do **not** build an omnibus X2 by adding certification, instructor quality, career progression, reward, rotation, training, coaching, and every Renstra HR provision into one scale. That would maximize contextual face validity while damaging construct validity.

A safer path is:

1. retain the validated competence-development-practice core;
2. conduct content validation with Brimob subject-matter experts;
3. adapt wording/referents without changing the construct's theoretical domain;
4. if important Korbrimob development practices fall outside the construct, treat them as context or reconsider the construct rather than adding items ad hoc.

### Status

**LOCK CANDIDATE, conditional on instrument-lineage verification, permissions, translation, and content validation.** This is currently the strongest construct in the proposed model.

## 3. Affective Organizational Commitment (M)

### Authoritative definition

`SRC-016` (Allen & Meyer, 1990) defines the affective component as employees' **emotional attachment to, identification with, and involvement in the organization**. It is conceptually distinct from:

- continuance commitment, based on perceived costs of leaving;
- normative commitment, based on obligation to remain.

### Critical distinction for Korbrimob

Renstra language about **jiwa korsa, disiplin, loyalitas, dedikasi, dan patuh hukum** is not a substitute for AOC measurement. Loyalty/dedication may overlap phenomenologically but can also reflect normative duty, professional identity, command compliance, or institutional socialization.

### Rival/adjacent employee-state constructs

- employee/work engagement;
- organizational identification;
- perceived organizational support;
- trust in leader/organization;
- public service motivation;
- job involvement;
- normative commitment.

### Measurement maturity

AOC has a mature measurement tradition. The measurement problem is therefore **not** that a scale is unavailable. The substantive risk is choosing AOC simply because it is easy to measure or appears in prior studies.

Before instrument lock:

1. select an exact validated AOC scale/version;
2. verify item-use and translation permissions;
3. avoid wording that collapses affective attachment into duty/loyalty;
4. test discriminant validity against conceptually nearby states if any are measured;
5. use collection procedures that reduce command-related social desirability.

### Status

**MEASURABLE BUT RETENTION UNDER REVIEW.** Issue #11 must establish why AOC is a better mechanism for the documented Korbrimob problem than engagement, trust, organizational identification, perceived organizational support, or public-service motivation.

## 4. Digital Competence / Digital Skills at Work (W)

### Working definition problem

The current title uses `Kompetensi Digital Personel`, but literature contains several adjacent concepts:

- digital skills;
- digital literacy;
- digital competence;
- digital self-efficacy;
- technology readiness;
- digital mindset;
- ICT competence;
- task-specific technical competence.

These are not interchangeable.

### Strong workplace measurement candidate

`SRC-017` registers Audrin, Audrin, and Salamin (2024), who developed a workplace-specific digital-skills scale with **52 items across eight dimensions**:

1. technology use;
2. cybersecurity;
3. content management;
4. communication and collaboration;
5. critical inquiry;
6. responsibility;
7. well-being;
8. identity/development.

This is a major advantage over generic citizen/youth digital-literacy frameworks because the dissertation is about capability **at work**.

### Fit problem in Brimob

The scale may be simultaneously **too broad and too generic** for Korbrimob. The Renstra and Issue #9 show specialized operational functions using different technologies and technical systems. For example, Jibom, Wanteror, KBRN, intelligence, communications, unmanned systems, and tactical surveillance have distinct capability profiles.

A respondent with low exposure to a given digital task could score low because the job does not require it—not because of low competence. This creates construct contamination by **opportunity/task exposure**.

### Measurement burden

Fifty-two digital items, combined with four other constructs and demographics, is likely too burdensome for an operational personnel survey. Shortening the scale cannot be done merely for convenience. It requires psychometric/content justification and may require a validated short form or a carefully validated context-specific subset.

### Rival/adjacent constructs

- task-specific technology competence;
- digital self-efficacy;
- digital literacy;
- technology readiness;
- technology access/infrastructure;
- digital task intensity;
- technology use frequency.

### Status

**REVISE/BOUND BEFORE LOCK.** Keep the capability domain open, but do not lock a universal `Digital Competence` construct or its moderator role until the population and common digital task requirements are established. A role-bounded population or an explicit digital-task-intensity measure may be necessary.

## 5. Individual Work Performance (Y)

### Construct definition

`SRC-018` establishes Individual Work Performance (IWP) as an employee-level performance construct rather than an organizational KPI. The IWPQ lineage converges on three generic dimensions:

1. task performance;
2. contextual performance;
3. counterproductive work behavior.

`SRC-012` provides Indonesian evidence: two independent samples totaling 609 workers supported the three-factor Indonesian IWPQ structure and criterion relation with work engagement. Earlier Indonesian adaptation/Rasch work provides additional psychometric support but also shows that individual items can behave differently across samples.

### Rival/adjacent constructs

- adaptive performance;
- operational readiness;
- unit/team performance;
- organizational performance;
- formal personnel appraisal;
- task performance alone;
- contextual performance/OCB.

### Key Brimob problem

A generic IWPQ is attractive precisely because Korbrimob contains many job types, but high-risk public-security work may contain adaptive, safety, command-compliance, and operational-readiness requirements not fully represented by generic IWPQ wording.

That does **not** justify casually adding an adaptive-performance fourth dimension. Instead:

1. test content validity of the existing IWP dimensions with Brimob experts;
2. decide whether Y is generic IWP or a more context-specific performance construct;
3. if adaptive performance is theoretically necessary, treat it as a separately justified construct/domain and validate it;
4. determine performance source through Issue #9 access evidence.

### Measurement source

If feasible, supervisor or administrative performance evidence is preferable for reducing same-source bias, but this preference is contingent on construct validity and access. A formal appraisal is not automatically better than a validated self-report if the appraisal measures a different construct.

### Status

**LOCK CANDIDATE AT THE CONSTRUCT LEVEL; MEASUREMENT SOURCE CONDITIONAL.** IWP is a coherent HRM outcome, but its exact content and source remain open.

# Cross-construct discriminant audit

| Pair | Main overlap risk | Required safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| X1 TL vs X2 competence-development practices | Individualized consideration/development by a leader can resemble individualized developmental support | X1 referent = immediate leader behavior; X2 referent = organization-provided development practices/opportunities. Avoid duplicate coaching/development wording across scales. |
| X2 development practices vs W digital competence | Both can use the word `competence` | X2 = organizational input/practice; W = individual's capability. Never use training/certification achievement as a digital-competence indicator. |
| M AOC vs Y contextual performance | Highly committed employees may display discretionary/contextual behavior | Keep emotional attachment/identification items separate from helping/initiative/performance behaviors; assess discriminant validity empirically. |
| M AOC vs loyalty/duty | Security institutions strongly socialize loyalty and duty | Avoid treating duty, obedience, or obligation as affective attachment; preserve affective vs normative distinction. |
| W digital competence vs digital task exposure | Low technology use can reflect job design rather than low capability | Measure/bound digital task requirements and access; do not infer competence from use frequency alone. |
| Y IWP vs organizational performance | Renstra KPIs are organizational/program metrics | Keep Y employee-level; do not use organizational targets as IWP indicators. |

# Translation and adaptation requirements

For all retained scales:

1. verify copyright/licensing/permission before reproducing full items;
2. use a documented translation/adaptation process rather than informal translation;
3. conduct expert content review for semantic and occupational fit;
4. conduct cognitive interviews/pilot testing with personnel comparable to the final population;
5. test measurement structure, reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity in the study context;
6. avoid deleting items solely because they reduce statistical fit unless the content consequence is examined;
7. if unit/function groups are compared, consider whether measurement equivalence/invariance is required.

# Respondent-burden implication

The current candidate measurement architecture can become too long, especially if:

- MLQ 45 items is chosen for X1;
- a 52-item workplace digital-skills scale is chosen for W;
- multidimensional X2 and IWP measures are also used.

Respondent burden is not merely logistical in this locus: operational personnel availability, fatigue, nonresponse, and satisficing can become measurement-quality problems. Therefore **brevity is a legitimate design criterion, but never a justification for unvalidated item deletion**.
