# Theory and Rival-Mechanism Audit

Status: **WORKING THEORY AUDIT — NOT A FINAL THEORY OR MODEL LOCK**  
Related Issue: #11  
Primary locus guideline: SRC-007 (Renstra Korbrimob Polri 2025–2029)  
Construct/field inputs: Issue #8 audit; Issue #9 desk audit; Issue #10 construct/measurement audit

## 1. Purpose

This audit asks which explanatory logic best connects the documented Korbrimob HRM problem to the proposed employee-level constructs. It deliberately does not choose a theory because it is fashionable or because it can be retrofitted around the working title.

The documented institutional problem is not a generalized performance crisis. The Renstra records continuing capability-development and modernization agendas while also identifying weaknesses in leadership, instructor professionalism, HR capability/technology mastery, professionalism/management, and operational effectiveness. The research therefore needs an explanation of **how organization- and leader-provided HR conditions are experienced by personnel and translated into individual work outcomes, and under what conditions that translation varies**.

## 4. Route-specific audit

### 4.1 Transformational leadership as X1

The **leadership domain** is well supported by the Renstra because leadership weakness is explicitly recorded. The remaining question is whether the academically appropriate representation is transformational leadership.

If Transformational Leadership is retained, SET can plausibly explain one pathway: supportive, developmental, inspirational, and individualized leader behavior can be interpreted as favorable treatment, encouraging reciprocal attachment and constructive behavior. OST further predicts that leader behavior is especially likely to influence organizational attachment when the supervisor is perceived as representing the organization.

However, the literature does not make AOC the unique mechanism. Park et al. and Jiatong et al. place engagement centrally; Bader et al. provide longitudinal evidence for need fulfilment, positive affect, and work engagement; public-sector evidence also indicates that direct leadership–performance effects can be weak or absent. Zhang (2026) provides recent direct evidence for partial mediation through AOC, but its cross-sectional design does not resolve mediator competition.

**Interim verdict:** retain Transformational Leadership as a **conditional construct candidate**, but the dissertation must justify why transformational behaviors match the specific leadership deficit described in Korbrimob. AOC is a defensible mediator for this route, but not uniquely established.

### 4.2 Competence Development Practices as X2

X2 remains the strongest construct–locus candidate. The Renstra directly documents Latharkatpuan, curriculum relevance, competence certification, instructor quality, personalized learning, rotation, and other developmental practices.

The strongest theoretical logic is not simply 'training increases performance'. An HRM-process/SET account is more defensible: personnel experience organizational development practices; those practices can signal organizational investment and support; favorable interpretation may strengthen attachment and reciprocation; performance may also improve through capability gains independent of AOC.

This implies two non-exclusive routes:

1. a **capability route**, because development can improve knowledge/skills/competence;
2. an **attitudinal exchange route**, because development can signal investment and support, strengthening AOC.

Moreira et al. (2024) directly support competence-development practices and AOC, although their outcome is turnover intention. Hasinat et al. (2026) support a broader perceived-HRM-practices to AOC to multidimensional-performance chain but do not isolate competence development.

**Interim verdict:** X2 is a **conditional LOCK candidate**. If retained, the dissertation should not assume AOC fully explains its relationship with performance; a direct/capability path should remain empirically testable.

### 4.3 Affective Organizational Commitment as M

The theory audit changes the interpretation of AOC relative to the locus-only audit.

- **Locus evidence:** still weak/directly absent. The Renstra does not diagnose low affective commitment and in fact identifies esprit de corps, discipline, loyalty, and dedication as organizational strengths.
- **Construct evidence:** strong. AOC is conceptually mature and measurable.
- **Mechanism evidence:** moderate-to-strong. Recent and earlier studies support AOC as an intervening mechanism between leadership/HR practices and employee outcomes.
- **Uniqueness:** weak. Engagement, POS, trust, meaningfulness, self-efficacy, identification, and affective/motivational mechanisms can produce similar observed patterns.

Zhang (2026) materially strengthens the direct empirical plausibility of TL–AOC–performance. Hasinat et al. (2026) similarly supports HR-practices–AOC–performance. Yet Park et al. (2022), Jiatong et al. (2022), and Bader et al. show that engagement/affect processes can be at least as theoretically plausible.

**Interim verdict:** upgrade AOC from 'plausible but weakly grounded in the locus' to **CONDITIONAL RETAIN — theoretically and empirically defensible, but not yet uniquely necessary**. It should survive Issue #12 only if the dissertation explicitly frames AOC as an employee-attitude black-box mechanism and acknowledges/controls the rival-mechanism problem in the literature review.

### 4.4 Individual Work Performance as Y

Individual Work Performance remains a defensible HRM outcome because the research question concerns translation of HR and leadership conditions to personnel-level behavior. The construct is also measurable with a validated Indonesian IWPQ architecture.

The main theoretical caution is level of analysis. Renstra indicators are program/organizational indicators and cannot substitute for employee-level IWP. The theory audit therefore supports Y but does not resolve the measurement-source question (self-rating, supervisor rating, administrative appraisal, or combination).

**Interim verdict:** **conditional LOCK candidate at construct level**, pending Issue #9 access and Issue #10 final measurement decision.

### 4.5 Digital Competence as W

Digital capability is institutionally relevant. The Renstra identifies technology-communication mastery weaknesses and technology/data-analytics capability priorities. The problem is not relevance of the construct; the problem is the **proposed moderation role**.

The strongest recent moderation evidence reviewed here follows a more specific mechanism:

- workplace digitalization or digital-technology requirements create demands;
- employees appraise those demands differently;
- digital literacy/competence operates as a personal resource shaping challenge/threat appraisal and downstream outcomes.

That pattern is supported by Zhan & Xie (2025) in public organizations and Li et al. (2026) in a three-wave design. Xu et al. (2025) further shows that employee effects of digital technologies are heterogeneous across contexts.

None of these studies establishes that digital competence moderates the generic relationship between AOC and individual performance. A conceptual argument can be constructed using **ability–motivation complementarity**: even highly committed personnel may translate willingness into performance less effectively when a task requires digital capability they do not possess. But this argument is only defensible when digital task requirements are substantively present. AOC also should not simply be relabeled 'motivation' to claim a full AMO model.

**Interim verdict:** Digital Competence is a **RETAINED CONSTRUCT CANDIDATE but an UNRESOLVED/WEAK MODERATOR in the current architecture**.

For W to remain a moderator of AOC–performance, at least one of the following must be true:

1. the sample is restricted to roles where digital technology is a material component of performance; or
2. digital task intensity / technology requirements are measured and incorporated as a boundary condition; or
3. strong field evidence demonstrates that digital capability is a pervasive performance requirement across the sampled Korbrimob population.

If none is satisfied, safer alternatives are:

- model Digital Competence as an employee capability/main effect on IWP;
- model it in a digital-demand-specific interaction rather than AOC–performance;
- or remove it from the core explanatory model while retaining it as a contextual/descriptive variable.

## 6. Falsification / failure conditions

The preferred account must be considered weakened if future evidence shows any of the following:

1. Field interviews/documentary evidence indicate that the Renstra leadership weakness concerns command/control compliance, tactical coordination, or supervisory discipline with little correspondence to transformational behaviors.
2. Competence-development practices are centrally assigned with minimal between-person exposure/perception variance, making an individual-perception X2 model empirically weak.
3. AOC shows little variation or poor discriminant validity against loyalty/esprit de corps, engagement, or identification in the Brimob context.
4. Supervisor-rated or administrative IWP is inaccessible and self-reported IWP is judged too sensitive or socially desirable to provide credible variance.
5. Digital task intensity is low or highly uneven in the sampled population and cannot be bounded/controlled.
6. Closest empirical studies consistently indicate that another mechanism explains the X1/X2–performance link more directly or robustly than AOC.

## 7. Preliminary theoretical recommendation

### 7.1 Leading backbone

**Social Exchange Theory, supported by Organizational Support/HRM-process logic, is currently the leading theoretical backbone for explaining why leader behavior and competence-development practices may translate into affective organizational commitment and constructive work outcomes.**

Qualification: SET is not currently sufficient to explain the digital-competence moderation. It should not be presented as a universal theory for every path.

### 7.2 Role of AMO

AMO may be used only as a **secondary ability–motivation logic** if required to discuss capability and willingness. The dissertation should not claim to test the full AMO framework unless Opportunity is explicitly operationalized. AOC is an organizational attachment attitude and must not be treated as equivalent to motivation without qualification.

### 7.3 Status of AOC

AOC now has enough recent empirical support to remain a serious mediator candidate. Its remaining weakness is locus-specific necessity and competition from engagement/POS/identification, not lack of empirical precedent.

### 7.4 Status of Digital Competence moderation

The current AOC-by-Digital-Competence moderation is **not ready to lock**. The best verified evidence supports digital literacy as a boundary condition of explicitly digital demands/digitalization. The moderation can be retained only with a defensible task-demand boundary or population restriction.

## 8. Model candidates to carry into Issue #12

### Candidate A — conservative HRM black-box model

Retain Transformational Leadership and Competence Development Practices as antecedents; retain AOC as mediator; retain direct relationships with IWP; treat Digital Competence as a main-effect capability or control/boundary descriptor until field evidence justifies interaction.

**Advantages:** strongest alignment with current evidence; avoids unsupported moderation.  
**Risk:** reduces the originally proposed novelty/conditional-process architecture.

### Candidate B — conditional moderated-mediation model

Same core model as Candidate A, but retain Digital Competence as a boundary condition on the AOC–IWP link **only for personnel whose work materially requires digital capability**, with digital-task intensity measured or the population restricted accordingly.

**Advantages:** preserves the proposed conditional-performance logic.  
**Risk:** currently rests more on theoretical ability–motivation complementarity than direct empirical precedent; field design must support it.

### Candidate C — digital-demand re-specification

Retain the HRM/AOC core but reposition digital competence in a digital-demand-specific mechanism, for example as a boundary condition of digital task requirements/digitalized work on appraisal/performance.

**Advantages:** closest fit to recent digital-literacy moderation evidence.  
**Risk:** materially changes the working title/model and adds at least one construct.

## 9. Implication for the background manuscript

The background can now make a more precise unresolved-problem statement:

- Korbrimob has documented leadership, competence-development, and technology-capability concerns;
- HRM and leadership research suggests such organizational inputs do not translate automatically into individual performance but operate through employee interpretations, attitudes, capabilities, and other psychological mechanisms;
- AOC is a defensible candidate mechanism, but evidence also supports engagement, support, meaningfulness, and other rival paths;
- digital capability is relevant, but whether it conditions the attitude–performance translation depends on the digital demands of the actual roles studied.

The final background should therefore present **mechanism uncertainty and boundary-condition uncertainty** as part of the research problem, rather than claim that mediation/moderation are already established facts.
