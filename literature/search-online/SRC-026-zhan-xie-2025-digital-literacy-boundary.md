## Evidence retained
The study used a three-wave design with 290 employees from public organizations in Guangdong, China. Workplace digitalization was linked to work engagement through challenge and threat appraisals. Digital literacy moderated the effects of perceived workplace digitalization on those appraisals.

## Research use
This is strong evidence that digital literacy can operate as a boundary condition in a public-sector setting. Crucially, the moderation is theoretically tied to an explicitly digital antecedent and cognitive appraisal mechanism. This pattern is more specific than treating digital competence as a generic moderator of any attitude–performance relationship.

## Limits
The outcome is work engagement, not individual work performance; the moderator is digital literacy rather than the exact working digital-competence construct; and the tested interaction is workplace digitalization by digital literacy, not AOC by digital competence. It therefore limits rather than directly supports the proposed AOC–performance moderation.
