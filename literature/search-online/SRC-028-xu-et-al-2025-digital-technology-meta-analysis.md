## Evidence retained
The meta-analysis synthesized 106 empirical studies and 119 effect sizes. Workplace digital technologies showed a mixed or 'double-edged' pattern: positive relationships were reported for outcomes such as task performance, innovation performance, engagement, job satisfaction, and efficacy, while adverse relationships appeared for burnout, anxiety, withdrawal, and other negative outcomes. Industry characteristics, technology-use type, and demographic factors explained part of the heterogeneity.

## Research use
The synthesis supports treating digitalization effects as contingent rather than uniformly beneficial. It strengthens the need to model task/technology context explicitly before assigning digital competence a universal role in the Sudianto model.

## Limits
The paper concerns digital-technology exposure and heterogeneous employee outcomes, not specifically digital competence as a moderator. It cannot establish the proposed commitment-by-digital-competence interaction.
