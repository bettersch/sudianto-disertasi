## Stage 1 evidence boundary

Registered Stage 1 evidence establishes organizational activities, priorities, officially documented weaknesses/problems, and stated capability targets. It does not establish:

- the prevalence or magnitude of those weaknesses across individual personnel;
- individual personnel perceptions of leadership;
- individual exposure to competence-development practices;
- affective organizational commitment levels;
- organization-wide digital competence levels;
- individual work-performance levels;
- causal effects of training, leadership, technology capability, commitment, or other constructs;
- or a scholarly research gap.

The Renstra directly strengthens the institutional case for leadership, HR capability/development, technology mastery, performance management, and professionalism as relevant problem domains. It does **not** establish that the academic constructs Transformational Leadership, Affective Organizational Commitment, Digital Competence, or Individual Work Performance are necessarily the correct representations. Digital/technology evidence also remains potentially role-specific and must be tested rather than universalized.

## Evidence requirements before strengthening the framing

- Important locus claims require a registered institutional/public source with an actual locator and access status.
- Important scholarly claims require verified source metadata and source notes; abstracts support only what they actually report.
- A gap is not established by a new location, a new variable combination, or the phrase “few studies.”
- Theory is not selected because it appears compatible with the working title; competing explanations must be inspected.
- Method is not selected because it was used in prior studies or requested in prior conversation; it follows the research problem and RQs.
- The digital-competence moderation proposition requires specific theoretical, contextual, role/task, and field support and may be revised if the evidence does not sustain it.

## Scope boundary

- Korbrimob or its personnel have generally low performance;
