# Source Note

- SRC ID: SRC-011
- Related Issue: #15
- Type: peer-reviewed journal article
- Citation: Wanto, D. G., & Kusumatuti, R. (2023). Analysis of Training, Competency, and Organizational Culture on Personnel Performance with the Mediation of Affective Commitment. *International Journal of Economics, Business and Accounting Research, 7*(2).
- DOI: 10.29040/ijebar.v7i2.9073
- Access: Open Access; publisher article and PDF verified; accessed 2026-10-11.

## Evidence retained
The study analyzed data from 206 Semarang–Ungaran police personnel using SEM-PLS. Training, competence, and organizational culture were positively associated with affective commitment. Affective commitment, training, and organizational culture were positively associated with job performance, while competence had no significant direct association with job performance.

## Research use
Provides directly relevant Indonesian police-context evidence that development-related conditions, affective commitment, and personnel performance can be empirically related. The null direct competence-performance result is important because it cautions against assuming that competence or development inputs translate automatically into performance.

## Limits
Training and competence are not equivalent to the dissertation’s candidate construct of organizational competence-development practices. Cross-sectional SEM-PLS does not establish causal mediation. The study is contextual support, not decisive model validation.
