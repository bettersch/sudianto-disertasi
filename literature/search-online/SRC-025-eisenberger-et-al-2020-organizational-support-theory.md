## Evidence retained
Organizational Support Theory (OST) explains how employees form a generalized perception that the organization values their contribution and cares about their well-being. The review identifies fairness, leader support, HR practices, and work conditions as major antecedents of perceived organizational support, particularly when employees see those actions as discretionary organizational choices.

## Research use
OST provides a theoretically precise rival/companion account for why leadership and competence-development practices may generate favorable employee attitudes. It implies that perceived organizational support may intervene before affective commitment, or may compete with AOC as the more proximal employee interpretation of organizational investment.

## Limits
POS is not currently part of the working model. Adding it simply because OST is strong would expand the model without locus-specific necessity. The review supports a mechanism that must be compared with, not automatically added to, AOC.
