## Evidence retained
The study developed and validated a workplace-specific digital-skills instrument. The final scale comprises 52 items across eight dimensions: technology use, cybersecurity, content management, communication and collaboration, critical inquiry, responsibility, well-being, and identity/development. Scale development proceeded through literature-based framework/item generation, expert review/piloting, and empirical validation with a large employee sample (reported n=923 in the study).

## Research use
Provides a strong workplace-oriented measurement anchor if Digital Competence/Digital Skills survives the model lock. It is preferable to generic population digital-literacy frameworks when the dissertation's interest is employee capability at work.

## Limits
- Fifty-two items impose substantial respondent burden in a five-construct dissertation survey.
- Several dimensions may be broader than the digital tasks required in specific Brimob roles.
- A common organization-wide digital construct is not defensible until Issue #9 establishes sufficiently common digital-task demands.
- This scale does not validate Digital Competence as a moderator.
