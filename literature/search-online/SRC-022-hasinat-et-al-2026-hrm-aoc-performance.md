## Evidence retained
The study surveyed 152 employees in Moroccan SMEs and used PLS-SEM. Perceived HR practices were positively associated with task, contextual, and adaptive performance and negatively associated with counterproductive work behavior. Affective commitment partially mediated the relationships between perceived HR practices and the performance dimensions. Transformational leadership was modeled as a moderator strengthening the HR-practice–performance relationships.

## Research use
This is useful recent evidence for the HRM-performance 'black box': employees' perceptions of HR practices can relate to multidimensional individual performance partly through affective commitment. It also demonstrates that leadership can be conceptualized as a boundary condition rather than only an antecedent, which is a relevant rival architecture to the Sudianto working model.

## Limits
The HR-practices construct is broader than competence-development practices, the sample is small and private-sector/SME based, and the design is cross-sectional. It therefore cannot be treated as direct evidence that Korbrimob competence-development practices operate through AOC or that transformational leadership has the same role in this locus.
